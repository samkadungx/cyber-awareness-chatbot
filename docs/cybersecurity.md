What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, devices, and data from unauthorized access, theft, or damage. It also focuses on keeping information safe, private, and available when you need it.



Today, almost everything relies on computers and the internet:



Communication – email, smartphones, tablets



Entertainment – video games, social media, apps



Transportation – navigation systems



Shopping – online stores, credit cards



Medicine – medical equipment, health records



Think about how much of your daily life uses technology. Now think about how much of your personal information is stored on your devices—or on someone else's system.



Why Does Cybersecurity Matter?

Without good cybersecurity, you face real risks, such as:



Malware erasing your entire system



An attacker breaking into your computer and changing files



Someone using your computer to attack others



Stolen credit card information and unauthorized purchases



Even with the best precautions, there is no guarantee these things won't happen. But you can take steps to reduce the chances.



Key Terms to Know

Hacker, attacker, or intruder

People who try to exploit weaknesses in software and computer systems for their own gain. Some do it out of curiosity, but their actions are usually against the intended use of the system. Results can range from harmless mischief to serious crimes like stealing or altering information.



Malicious code (malware)

Unwanted files or programs that harm a computer or compromise data. Types include viruses, worms, and Trojan horses.



Malware may:



Require you to do something first, like open an email attachment or visit a bad webpage



Spread on its own by exploiting software flaws, then infect other computers



Pretend to be one thing while secretly doing something else (e.g., a "speed up your PC" program that actually steals your data)



Vulnerabilities

Flaws in software, firmware, or hardware that attackers can exploit. They often come from programming errors. Attackers use these flaws to infect computers or perform other malicious actions.



How to Improve Your Cybersecurity

Follow these basic best practices:



Keep software up to date

Install patches so attackers cannot exploit known problems. Enable automatic updates if available.



Run up-to-date antivirus software

Good antivirus can detect, quarantine, and remove malware. Enable automatic updates. Note: antivirus relies on known patterns, so it may not catch brand-new threats like zero-day exploits.



Use strong passwords

Choose passwords that are hard to guess. Use different passwords for different programs and devices. Aim for at least 16 characters—long passphrases are best.



Change default usernames and passwords

Default login details are easy for attackers to find. Change them as soon as possible to something strong and unique.



Turn on multifactor authentication (MFA)

MFA uses at least two identity checks to verify you. Even if an attacker knows your username and password, MFA makes it much harder for them to get in.



Install a firewall

A firewall can block malicious traffic before it enters your system and restrict unnecessary outbound communications. Some operating systems include one—enable and configure it properly.



Be suspicious of unexpected emails

Phishing emails are one of the most common risks. Their goal is to steal information, steal money, or install malware. Treat all unexpected emails with caution.
